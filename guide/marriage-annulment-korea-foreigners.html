Annulling a Marriage in Korea: Void Marriage (혼인무효), Annulment (혼인취소) and the Three-Month Fraud Deadline

By Juan, Attorney at Law 8 min read English · Español · Português

Most marriages that fail end in divorce. Korean law also has a way to say that a marriage should never have existed, or can be undone because of what happened at the start. A marriage is void (혼인무효, honin muhyo) when there was never a real agreement to marry. It can be annulled (혼인취소, honin chwiso) on a short list of grounds, including fraud and duress. The deadlines for annulment are short, and the consequences are not the same as a divorce.

Foreign spouses usually come to this question in one of two ways: they discover after the wedding that the other person lied about something fundamental, or they suspect the marriage was only ever a route to a visa. This guide covers the two procedures, the deadlines, what happens to children and the family register, and how foreign law and immigration come into it.

→Void or Annulled: Two Different Cases

Void marriage (혼인무효)Annulment (혼인취소)
GroundsNo agreement to marry between the parties; marriages between certain close relatives or in-laws (Civil Act Art. 815)Under-age marriage or missing consent, prohibited relatives, bigamy, an undisclosed serious disease or other grave reason, fraud or duress (Art. 816)
Who can sueThe spouses, a legal representative, or relatives within the fourth degree, at any time (Family Litigation Act Art. 23)Depends on the ground (Civil Act Arts. 817, 818); for fraud or duress, the deceived or coerced spouse
DeadlineNone3 months for fraud or duress (Art. 823); 6 months for an undisclosed serious disease or grave reason (Art. 822)
Mediation first?No (a 가류 case)Yes, mediation is required first (a 나류 case, Family Litigation Act Arts. 2 and 50)
EffectThe marriage is treated as never validThe marriage ends from the judgment; it is not undone retroactively (Art. 824)

Both are decided by the family court. The case is filed where the spouses live or last lived together, following the order in Family Litigation Act Art. 22.

→“There Was Never a Real Marriage”: the Void-Marriage Ground

A marriage in Korea takes effect when it is registered (Civil Act Art. 812). Registration is a form, though, and Article 815(1) makes a marriage void when the parties did not agree to marry. This is the ground raised when one side used the registration only to obtain a visa, or never intended to live as a married couple.

  • The burden is on the person who says the marriage is void. Messages, the length and pattern of living together, what happened to the money, and when and how the other spouse left are the kind of facts the court looks at.
  • There is no deadline, but evidence gets harder to collect as time passes.
  • If the facts fit both, a void-marriage claim can be paired with a fallback claim for divorce, so that the case does not end with nothing if the court is not persuaded.

→Fraud and Duress: the Three-Month Clock

A spouse who married because of fraud or duress can ask the court to annul the marriage (Art. 816(3)). The right is lost three months after discovering the fraud, or three months after escaping the duress (Art. 823).

Not every untruth told before a wedding is enough. The court asks whether the lie concerned something that mattered to the decision to marry. Whether a particular lie meets that standard is decided case by case, which is why it is worth getting advice before the three months run out rather than after.

A separate ground covers a spouse who, at the time of the marriage, did not know of a serious disease or other grave reason that makes married life impossible to continue (Art. 816(2)). That right ends six months after learning of it (Art. 822).

Fraud gives you three months from the day you find out. After that, the question becomes divorce, not annulment.

→What Happens After the Judgment

  • Children of a void marriage are treated as born outside marriage (Civil Act Art. 855(1)), so the father's legal relationship to the child may need to be established separately. See birth registration in Korea.
  • Children of an annulled marriage: the divorce rules on custody, child support and visitation apply (Art. 824-2, applying Arts. 837 and 837-2).
  • Damages: a spouse at fault for the void or annulled marriage is liable for the financial loss and the emotional suffering caused (Art. 825, applying Art. 806). The Family Litigation Act lists these damages claims, including claims against a third party, as family court cases (Art. 2(1)(i)(c)(2)).
  • The family register: after a void-marriage judgment becomes final, the person who sued must apply to correct the register within one month (Act on the Registration of Family Relationships Art. 107). After an annulment, the judgment must be reported within one month of becoming final (Arts. 73 and 58).

→When One Spouse Is Foreign

Jurisdiction. Korean courts can hear a case about a marriage if, for example, one spouse habitually lives in Korea and Korea was the couple's last common habitual residence, or if the claimant and a minor child habitually live in Korea (Private International Law Act Art. 56(1)).

Which law decides validity. Whether the requirements for a valid marriage were met is decided, for each spouse, by that spouse's own national law (Art. 63(1)). The form of the marriage follows the law of the place of marriage or either spouse's national law, but a marriage celebrated in Korea with a Korean spouse must follow Korean law (Art. 63(2)). A foreign spouse's own law can therefore matter to the outcome.

Immigration. A residence permit obtained by false or unlawful means can be cancelled (Immigration Act Art. 89(1)(ii)). Submitting false documents in an application to extend or change a stay is prohibited (Art. 26), punishable by up to 3 years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 30 million won (Art. 94(xvii-2)), and is a ground for deportation (Art. 46(1)(x-2)). Making a false marriage report so that it is entered in the family register can also be a crime: up to 5 years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 10 million won (Criminal Act Art. 228(1)).

If you are the foreign spouse and the other side is now saying your marriage was a sham, get advice before you give any statement to immigration officers or the police. In a criminal case the police investigate; under the system in force since 2 October 2026, prosecutors at the 공소청 decide whether to charge but no longer investigate or question suspects themselves. For your status after the marriage ends, see keeping an F-6 visa after the marriage ends.

→Annulment or Divorce?

If the deadline for annulment has passed, or the facts do not fit a void marriage, divorce is still available on the grounds in Civil Act Art. 840, and fault-based damages can be claimed in the divorce. See divorce in Korea as a foreigner and property division and 위자료.

→What to Do Now

  1. Write down the date you found out about the fraud, or the date the duress ended. The three months run from that day.
  2. Keep the evidence: messages, documents the other side gave you before the wedding, records of where each of you lived, and money transfers.
  3. Get your family relation certificate (혼인관계증명서) to confirm what is registered and when.
  4. Think about the visa at the same time if either spouse holds a marriage-based status.
  5. Decide on the claim: void marriage, annulment, divorce, or one of them with a fallback.
