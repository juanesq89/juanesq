A Kicked Car Mirror, a Smashed Phone: Property Damage (재물손괴) in Korea

By Juan, Attorney at Law 7 min read English · Español · Português

A car mirror kicked on the walk home. A phone thrown at the floor in an argument. A key dragged along a neighbour's car, a former partner's files deleted, a bike hidden to make a point. In Korea, damaging someone else's property on purpose is a crime: property damage (재물손괴, jaemul songoe), with up to 3 years in prison or a 7 million won fine.

Many foreigners assume this is a matter of paying for the damage. Paying matters a great deal, but it does not by itself stop a criminal case, and the line between a crime and a civil bill is not how much the thing cost. It is whether you meant to do it.

→What Article 366 Punishes

Article 366 of the Criminal Act punishes anyone who damages (손괴) or conceals (은닉) another person's property, documents or electronic records, or otherwise impairs their usefulness, with up to 3 years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 7 million won.

  • Breaking is not required. Hiding something, or making it unusable without breaking it, is covered by the words “conceals” and “otherwise impairs”.
  • Data counts. The article names electronic records (전자기록등 특수매체기록), so deleting or corrupting files that belong to someone else can fall under it.
  • It has to be someone else's. Damaging your own property that another person holds or has a right over, such as something pledged or leased out, is a different offence: obstruction of the exercise of rights (권리행사방해, Art. 323), up to 5 years or 7 million won.
  • Attempts are punished (Art. 371).

→Accidents Are a Bill, Not a Crime

Article 14 of the Criminal Act punishes negligence only where a statute says so, and the chapter on property damage has no negligence offence. Knocking over a display, dropping a borrowed camera or scraping a wall while moving furniture is a civil matter: you may owe the cost, but there is no 재물손괴 charge. Damage caused while driving is governed by separate traffic rules; see car accidents in Korea.

Intent, though, is read from the circumstances. An argument just before, the words used, repeated blows, the CCTV angle: these are what the police use to decide whether “it was an accident” holds up. And being drunk does not help. Article 10(3) of the Criminal Act removes the usual reduction for impaired judgment from someone who foresaw the danger and got into that state voluntarily.

The line between a crime and a repair bill is not the price of the thing. It is whether you meant to do it.

→Paying for the Damage

Some minor offences in Korea cannot be prosecuted against the victim's express wish, simple assault for example (Art. 260(3)). Article 366 is not one of them. The victim's forgiveness does not legally end the case.

In practice, though, repairing the loss is the most useful thing you can do. A victim who has been paid and states in writing that they do not want you punished (처벌불원) is something the prosecutor and the court weigh heavily. Depending on the facts, a case can end in a suspended indictment (기소유예) or a summary fine order (약식명령) rather than a trial. How to write the settlement so it does that job is in criminal settlement agreements.

The civil debt exists either way. Under the Civil Act you must compensate the damage you caused intentionally or negligently (Art. 750). The owner's claim runs for 3 years from knowing of the damage and who caused it, and at most 10 years from the act (Art. 766).

→If the Police Contact You

Since 2 October 2026 the prosecutor at the public prosecution office (공소청) decides whether to indict on the basis of the police file and does not question suspects, so the police interview is the statement that counts.

  1. Get a repair estimate or receipt. A number on paper turns an argument into a settlement conversation.
  2. Do not pressure the owner. Offer to pay through a clear message or a lawyer, not by turning up at their door.
  3. Keep your own evidence: messages before and after, video that shows how it happened. If it was an accident, this is what shows it.
  4. Give one consistent account. If someone was also pushed or hurt, the case may include an assault charge; see assault charges in Korea. Your rights in the interview are in questioned by Korean police.
  5. Think about your visa. An open case can lead to a departure ban (출국정지), and a conviction can affect renewals or lead to deportation proceedings.

The limitation period for prosecution is 5 years for Article 366 and 7 years for the aggravated offence in Article 369(1) (Criminal Procedure Act Art. 249(1)).

→If It Was Your Property

Photograph the damage before anything is repaired, get a written estimate, and ask the shop, the building or the car owner nearby to keep any CCTV or dashcam footage. Report it to the police (112, or the local station) if you want a criminal case. To recover the money, you can claim directly from the person, and for smaller amounts the small claims procedure is built for exactly this.
