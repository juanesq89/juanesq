Dividing Property in a Korean Divorce (재산분할) — and Why Alimony (위자료) Is a Different Claim

By Juan, Attorney at Law 8 min read English · Español · Português

In English “alimony” usually means monthly support after a divorce. Korean law cuts the money questions differently. Property division (재산분할, jaesan bunhal) splits what the two of you built. 위자료 (wijaryo), usually translated as alimony, is compensation from the spouse whose fault ended the marriage. Child support is a third claim. Each has its own rule, and one of them disappears two years after the divorce.

Foreign spouses tend to discover this late: a consensual divorce can be registered without any agreement on property, and nothing reminds you afterwards that the right to ask for a share is running out. This guide covers what the family court divides, how hidden or transferred assets are dealt with, how 위자료 works, and which country's law and courts come into it when one or both of you are foreign.

→Three Claims, Three Rules

ClaimWhat it isWhere the rule is
Property division (재산분할)A share of the property built through both spouses' cooperationCivil Act Art. 839-2; applied to court divorces by Art. 843. Ends 2 years after the divorce
위자료 (compensation for fault)Damages from the spouse whose fault caused the divorce, including for emotional sufferingCivil Act Art. 806, applied to court divorces by Art. 843
Child support (양육비)The cost of raising a minor child, decided with custodyCivil Act Art. 837. See unpaid child support in Korea

Property division does not depend on fault. A spouse who caused the breakdown can still ask for a share of what was built, and a spouse who receives 위자료 still needs a separate property claim.

→What the Court Divides

If the spouses cannot agree, the family court sets the amount and the method of division, taking into account the property formed through both spouses' cooperation and all other circumstances (Civil Act Art. 839-2(2)).

  • Whose name it is in does not settle it. During the marriage, property a spouse owned before marrying, or acquired in their own name, is that spouse's separate property (Art. 830(1)). Division asks a different question: what the couple built together. A flat registered only to one spouse can still be part of it.
  • Property of unclear ownership is presumed to be jointly owned (Art. 830(2)).
  • Work at home counts as cooperation. The article speaks of cooperation, not of salaries, so the spouse who raised the children or ran the household is not limited to what they earned.
  • Assets abroad are part of the picture of what the couple built, but enforcing a Korean order against property in another country depends on that country's rules. Plan for that before you file, not after.

The court can order each spouse to file a list of their assets (Family Litigation Act Art. 48-2) and, if the lists are not enough, can make asset inquiries to banks and public bodies in the spouses' names (Art. 48-3). Ask for these early when you suspect the other side is understating what they hold.

Fault decides 위자료. Cooperation decides the property. Two years decides whether you can still ask.

→The Two-Year Deadline

The right to claim property division is extinguished two years after the date of the divorce (Civil Act Art. 839-2(3)). This is not a deadline that can be paused by sending messages or negotiating. If your consensual divorce was registered without a property agreement, the two years have already started.

The same clock applies after a court divorce (Art. 843). In practice property is usually claimed in the divorce case itself. Family Litigation Act Art. 50 requires most family disputes, including property division and 위자료, to go to mediation (조정) first. If you file a claim directly, the court normally refers it to mediation.

→If Assets Are Being Moved or Hidden

  1. Freeze them first. The family court can order a provisional attachment or injunction with the property division case as the main case, and it can do so without requiring security (Family Litigation Act Art. 63). See provisional attachment in Korea for how freezing orders work.
  2. Undo a transfer made to defeat your claim. If a spouse disposes of property knowing it will harm your property division claim, you can ask the family court to cancel the transfer and restore the property (Civil Act Art. 839-3). This must be filed within 1 year of learning of the transfer and within 5 years of the transfer itself (Art. 839-3(2), applying Art. 406(2)).
  3. Keep records now: bank statements, property registry extracts, pension and insurance statements, business records, and screenshots of accounts abroad. Records are easier to obtain before the other side knows a case is coming.

→위자료: Compensation for the Fault That Ended the Marriage

Article 843 applies Article 806 to court divorces: the spouse at fault is liable for the damage caused, and that liability covers emotional suffering as well as financial loss (Art. 806(2)). The fault is usually one of the grounds for a court divorce in Article 840, such as an affair, malicious desertion, or seriously unjust treatment.

  • A third person can be sued too. The Family Litigation Act lists claims for damages caused by a divorce, including claims against a third party, as family court cases (Art. 2(1)(i)(c)(2)). This is how a claim against a spouse's affair partner is brought.
  • It is personal. The claim for emotional suffering cannot be transferred or inherited unless an agreement on it has been made or a suit has been filed (Art. 806(3)).
  • The amount depends on the evidence. Keep messages, photos and records of the conduct you rely on, and how you obtained them. Recording your own conversations is lawful; recording other people's private conversations is not (see recording conversations in Korea).

→Which Court, and Which Country's Law

Korean courts can hear a divorce if, for example, one spouse habitually lives in Korea and Korea was the couple's last common habitual residence, or if the claimant and a minor child habitually live in Korea (Private International Law Act Art. 56(1)).

The governing law is a separate question. For divorce, Article 66 applies the ladder in Article 64: first the law of the spouses' common nationality, then the law of their common habitual residence, then the law of the place most closely connected to them. If one spouse is a Korean national habitually resident in Korea, Korean law applies. Two foreigners of the same nationality divorcing in Seoul may therefore find their own country's law applied by the Korean court.

Spouses can choose the law for their matrimonial property in a dated, signed written agreement, limited to the law of either spouse's nationality, either spouse's habitual residence, or, for real estate, where it is located (Art. 65(2)). A marital property contract made under foreign law can be relied on against third parties in Korea if it is registered here (Art. 65(4)). See prenups in Korea.

→What to Do Now

  1. Before signing a consensual divorce, put the property agreement in writing, or know that you are leaving it for a claim you must file within two years.
  2. Already divorced? Check the date the divorce took effect and count two years from it.
  3. List what you know about: property in Korea, accounts and property abroad, pensions, a business, debts.
  4. If assets are moving, ask about a freezing order before anything else.
  5. Think about your visa at the same time. A spouse on a marriage visa should read keeping an F-6 visa after the marriage ends.
