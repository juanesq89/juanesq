An Edited Bank Statement, a Borrowed ID Card: Forged Documents (문서위조) in Korea

By Juan, Attorney at Law 7 min read English · Español · Português

A bank balance edited to reach a visa threshold. An employment letter the employer never signed. A friend’s alien registration card used to open a phone line. In Korea a forged or borrowed document is a criminal case, and when it goes into a visa file it is an immigration case as well.

Most forgery cases involving foreign residents do not begin with a printing press. They begin with a PDF: a balance changed before it is printed, a contract typed up on a company’s letterhead, a certificate “fixed” by an agent who promised a faster visa. Korean law reaches these from two directions. The Criminal Act punishes making and using the document; the Immigration Act punishes putting it in an application.

→What the Criminal Act Punishes

Chapter 20 of the Criminal Act (문서에 관한 죄) separates documents of a public official or public office from private documents. Forgery requires that the document be made with the purpose of using it (행사할 목적), and using a forged document is a separate offence with the same penalty as making it.

  • Copies count. A photocopy or fax of a document is treated as the document itself (Art. 237-2). Altering a copy and handing that in is not a loophole.
  • Attempts are punished for Articles 225 to 234 (Art. 235). Being caught before the document was accepted does not end the case.
  • A doctor’s note. A doctor who writes a false medical certificate faces up to 3 years, suspension of qualifications of up to 7 years or a fine of up to 30 million won (Art. 233), and the person who knowingly uses it faces the same penalty (Art. 234).
  • Your own form, your own name. Article 231 is about another person’s document. A false statement in an application you sign yourself is usually not forgery under that article, but in an immigration application it is covered by the Immigration Act, below.

→In a Visa File, the Immigration Act Applies Too

Article 26 of the Immigration Act prohibits, in applications for permits granted inside Korea (activities outside your status, a change or addition of workplace, the grant, change or extension of a status of stay, among others), submitting forged or altered documents as evidence or filing an application that contains false facts, and also arranging or encouraging it. Article 7-2 covers the step before entry: inviting a foreigner through false statements or a false guarantee, and applying falsely for a visa or a visa issuance certificate (사증발급인정서), or arranging either.

  • Criminal penalty: up to 3 years or a fine of up to 30 million won for either (Art. 94, items 3 and 17-2).
  • Deportation ground: a foreigner who violates Article 26, or who violates Article 7-2 or entered Korea through such a false invitation, can be deported (Art. 46(1), items 2 and 10-2). So can a foreigner released after a sentence of imprisonment, with or without labor (item 13).
  • Permit cancelled: a visa or stay permit obtained by false or other unlawful means can be cancelled or changed (Art. 89(1)(ii)). If the Ministry calls you in to hear your side, the reasons, date and place must be notified at least 7 days in advance (Art. 89(2)–(3)).
  • Coming back: entry can be refused while fewer than 5 years have passed since you left under a deportation order (Art. 11(1)(vi)).

The papers an agent prepared are still the papers you signed and handed in.

→Where These Cases Usually Come From

  • Bank balance certificates edited to reach the amount required for a student, job-seeker or family visa.
  • Employment certificates, payslips or contracts drafted by someone other than the employer for a change of status or an extension.
  • Diplomas, transcripts or criminal record checks altered for a teaching or skilled-work visa. An apostille confirms the signature and seal on a document; it does not make altered contents genuine.
  • Lease contracts made only so that an address can be registered.
  • A marriage registered only for the visa, with no intention of living as a married couple: investigated as a false entry in the official register (Art. 228(1)) and as a false invitation (Immigration Act Art. 7-2).
  • A friend’s or a stranger’s alien registration card used to open a phone line or an account, or to get past an age check: improper use of a public document (Art. 230). Lending your own card has its own risks, set out in lending your name or your account.

→If the Immigration Office or the Police Contact You

A doubtful document is usually noticed first at the immigration office, and an interview there can be the first step of a criminal case. Since 2 October 2026 the prosecutor at the public prosecution office (공소청) decides whether to indict on the basis of the investigators’ file and does not question suspects, so the first interview is the statement that counts.

  1. Do not quietly submit a “corrected” document or ask anyone to issue a back-dated one. A second document made to cover the first becomes a second charge.
  2. Keep every message with the agent or broker. If someone else prepared the papers, those messages show what you knew and what you paid for. Arranging false applications is itself an offence (Arts. 7-2 and 26).
  3. Find the genuine version. The real statement, contract or certificate shows what actually changed and why, and it matters for how the case is closed.
  4. Prepare before the interview and give one consistent account. Your rights are set out in questioned by Korean police.
  5. Think about your status. An open case can lead to a departure ban (출국정지), and a conviction or a cancelled permit can lead to deportation proceedings. If you need to change status lawfully, the steps are in changing your visa inside Korea.

The limitation period for prosecution follows the maximum penalty: 10 years for public-document forgery (Art. 225), 7 years for private-document forgery (Art. 231), and 5 years for the Immigration Act offences and for improper use (Arts. 230 and 236) (Criminal Procedure Act Art. 249(1)).
