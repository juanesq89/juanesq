Deepfake Sexual Images (딥페이크 · 허위영상물) in Korea: Making One Is a Crime, and So Is Watching It

By Juan, Attorney at Law 7 min read English · Español · Português

A face taken from someone's social media and placed on a sexual image. A "joke" made with an app and dropped into a group chat. A file someone forwarded to you that you never opened again. In Korea each of these can be a sexual offence, and the law does not care that the picture is fake. It cares that the person in it is real.

Korean law calls these files 허위영상물 (false images) and the media calls them 딥페이크 (deepfakes). They are prosecuted under the Act on Special Cases Concerning the Punishment of Sexual Crimes (성폭력범죄의 처벌 등에 관한 특례법, the "Sexual Crimes Act"), not under the general Criminal Act. This guide covers both sides: what to do if you are accused, and what to do if your face was used.

→What Article 14-2 Covers

Article 14-2(1) of the Sexual Crimes Act punishes anyone who edits, synthesises or processes a photograph, video or audio recording of a person's face, body or voice into a form that may cause sexual desire or shame, against the will of the person depicted. The penalty is up to 7 years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 50 million won.

Two points in that wording matter more than any other. First, the offence is complete when the image is made. The current text, as amended on 16 October 2024, punishes the editing itself; it does not require that the image be sent to anyone. Second, voice is included. A synthetic sexual audio clip built from someone's real voice falls under the same article as an edited picture.

→Making, Sharing, Keeping, Watching: Five Different Offences

ConductLawPenalty
Making the image (editing, synthesising, processing)Sexual Crimes Act Art. 14-2(1)Up to 7 years or ₩50M
Distributing, selling, providing or publicly showing it, including sharing later an image the person once agreed toArt. 14-2(2)Up to 7 years or ₩50M
Distributing it online for profitArt. 14-2(3)Imprisonment of at least 3 years, no fine option
Possessing, buying, storing or watching itArt. 14-2(4)Up to 3 years or ₩30M
Threatening someone with such an imageArt. 14-3(1)Imprisonment of at least 1 year
Using that threat to make the person do something, or stop them exercising a rightArt. 14-3(2)Imprisonment of at least 3 years

For habitual offenders the penalty for making or distributing can be increased by up to half (Art. 14-2(5)), and attempts are punishable (Art. 15). The fourth row is the one that surprises people: keeping or watching the file is a separate crime with its own penalty, so "I only received it" is not a defence in itself.

If the person shown is under 19, a different and much heavier law applies. The Act on the Protection of Children and Youth against Sex Offences covers material in which a minor, or a person or representation clearly recognisable as a minor, appears in sexual conduct (Art. 2(v)). Producing it carries life imprisonment or at least 5 years (Art. 11(1)); buying, possessing or watching it carries at least 1 year (Art. 11(5)). There is no fine option in either.

→"It Was Obviously Fake" and Other Arguments That Do Not Work

  • "Everyone could tell it was fake." The law punishes the edited image as an edited image. Whether a viewer would believe it is real is not part of the offence.
  • "I never sent it to anyone." Making it (Art. 14-2(1)) and keeping it (Art. 14-2(4)) are offences on their own.
  • "She agreed to the photo." Agreeing to be photographed is not agreeing to be edited into a sexual image. And even where the person agreed to the editing, sharing it later against their will is a separate offence (Art. 14-2(2)).
  • "The victim forgave me, so the case is over." Unlike a basic threat or assault, this offence has no rule that ends the prosecution when the victim no longer wants punishment. A settlement can reduce the sentence, but it does not close the file.

Police can also investigate these offences undercover, approaching suspects in online spaces without revealing that they are officers (Art. 22-2). Group chats and paid channels are where many of these cases begin.

The picture is fake. The person in it is real, and so is the criminal record.

→What a Conviction Brings Beyond the Sentence

  • Sex offender registration. A conviction or summary order for an offence under Articles 3 to 15 of the Sexual Crimes Act, which includes Article 14-2, makes you a registered person (Art. 42(1)). Unlike some lesser sexual offences, a fine does not avoid it. You must submit your personal information to the police within 30 days after the judgment becomes final (Art. 43(1)).
  • Travel reporting. A registered person who leaves Korea to stay abroad for 6 months or more must report the destination and period to the police beforehand, and report their return within 14 days (Art. 43-2).
  • Confiscation. Money earned from these offences, and property derived from it, is confiscated or its value collected (Art. 15-3, for offences committed after that article took effect in 2025).
  • Your visa. A criminal conviction is a ground on which residence is reviewed, extensions refused, or removal pursued, and a departure ban may already be in place during the investigation. For anyone on a teaching visa, a sexual offence conviction can also close off work with children. See deportation and entry bans.

→If You Are Accused

  1. Do not delete anything. The phone and cloud accounts will be examined forensically, deleted files are often recovered, and deletion after you learn of the investigation becomes an aggravating fact. How phone seizure works is explained in illegal filming charges in Korea.
  2. Find out which paragraph you are accused under. Making, distributing and possessing are different accusations with different penalties, and a case about one file can widen to everything on the device.
  3. Prepare for the police interview. Since 2 October 2026 the prosecutor at the public prosecution office (공소청) decides on indictment from the police file and does not question suspects, so the police statement is the one that counts. Your rights are in questioned by Korean police.
  4. Do not contact the person depicted yourself. An apology message can be read as pressure, and pressure using the image is a separate and heavier offence (Art. 14-3). Any settlement goes through a lawyer.

The limitation period for prosecution follows the maximum penalty: 7 years for making or distributing under Article 14-2(1) and (2), and 5 years for possessing or watching under Article 14-2(4) (Criminal Procedure Act Art. 249(1)(iv) and (v)). Where the victim was a minor, the period starts only when the victim turns adult (Sexual Crimes Act Art. 21(1)).

→If Your Face Was Used

  • Keep the evidence before it disappears. Save the links, the account names, the chat room name and the dates, with screenshots that show them. Do not download or forward the image itself to friends to "show" them.
  • Report it. You can go to the police directly or file a written complaint (고소, Criminal Procedure Act Art. 223); see filing a criminal complaint in Korea. If you are being threatened with the image, call 112.
  • You can have your own lawyer in the case. A victim's lawyer can attend your police interviews and the court hearings and inspect the court records (Sexual Crimes Act Art. 27). If you have no lawyer, the prosecutor may appoint a state-funded one, and must do so for a victim under 19 (Art. 27(6)).
  • Ask for removal early. Deletion support for these images is available through public bodies, and the earlier the report, the more copies can still be traced.
  • Compensation is a separate claim. Damages for the harm and distress are claimed in a civil action (Civil Act Arts. 750 and 751), alongside or after the criminal case. What victims can expect from the criminal process is in a foreign victim's guide to sexual offences in Korea.
