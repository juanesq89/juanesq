Riding an Electric Scooter (전동킥보드) in Korea: The Licence, Helmet and Drinking Rules Foreigners Miss

By Juan, Attorney at Law 7 min read English · Español · Português

The rental scooters parked on every corner in Seoul unlock with an app and a card. The app does not ask the question the law cares about first: whether you hold a licence that covers them.

Electric kick scooters (전동킥보드, jeondong kikboding) are treated by Korean law as vehicles, not as toys or bicycles with a battery. Most of the trouble foreign residents and visitors get into comes from that single point: the rules are those of a small motor vehicle, and the consequences of an accident are those of a traffic accident.

→What the Law Calls a "Personal Mobility Device"

The Road Traffic Act (도로교통법) defines a personal mobility device (개인형 이동장치) as a moped-class vehicle whose motor stops working above 25 km/h and which weighs under 30 kg (Article 2). The Enforcement Rule lists three kinds: electric kick scooters, two-wheeled self-balancing devices, and bicycles that can move on motor power alone.

Because these devices sit inside the legal category of mopeds (원동기장치자전거), the licence rules for mopeds apply to them. Because they are also grouped with bicycles as "bicycles etc." (자전거등), they share the bicycle rules about where you may ride.

→You Need a Licence That Covers Mopeds

Riding a scooter requires a Korean licence whose range includes mopeds. The entry-level one is the moped licence (원동기장치자전거면허), which cannot be issued to anyone under 16 (Article 82). Every Korean car licence — first-class or second-class ordinary — also covers mopeds under the Enforcement Rule.

A foreign licence on its own does not count. An International Driving Permit, or a foreign licence recognised under a bilateral arrangement, counts only if it is marked as valid for this category of vehicle (Article 156, item 13). Check the categories on your permit before you ride.

If you live in Korea and ride regularly, the practical answer is to convert your home licence or take the moped test. Converting a car licence gives you a Korean car licence, which already covers scooters.

→The Rules, and the Fine Notice for Each

Violations on a scooter can be handled with a fine notice (범칙금) issued by the police. The amounts below are those set for "bicycles etc." in the Enforcement Decree.

The passenger rule is strict for kick scooters: the Enforcement Rule sets the capacity at one person. Two friends on one rental scooter is a violation even for a short ride.

→Drinking: a Small Fine, a Large Licence Problem

Riding a scooter drunk is punished more lightly than drunk driving in a car: the criminal penalty is a fine of up to 200,000 won, detention or a petty fine (Article 156, item 11), and it can be handled with the fine notice above.

The part people miss is the licence. The provision that lets the police revoke or suspend a licence for drunk riding applies to anyone who rides a "motor vehicle etc." (자동차등) drunk — a category that includes mopeds, and therefore scooters (Articles 2 and 93). The suspension or revocation covers every class on your licence. A short ride home after drinking can cost you the car licence you need for work.

Refusing the breath test is its own violation, and it also appears on the list of grounds for revoking a licence.

→Where You May Ride

  • Bike lanes first. Where a bicycle road exists, scooters must use it (Article 13-2)
  • Otherwise, the right edge of the road. Not the middle of the lane, and not the sidewalk
  • Sidewalks only where signs allow it, or where the road itself cannot be used because of damage or works — and then slowly, stopping for pedestrians
  • At a crosswalk, get off and walk the scooter across (Article 13-2(6)) — the same rule that applies to bicycles

→If You Hit Someone

A collision that injures a pedestrian is a traffic accident under the Act on Special Cases Concerning the Settlement of Traffic Accidents (교통사고처리 특례법). For most accidents, negligent injury cannot be prosecuted against the victim's wishes, so a settlement can close the case.

That exit is closed in a list of exceptions (Article 3(2)), and three of them are exactly the situations scooters are involved in: riding without a licence (item 7), riding drunk (item 8), and riding on the sidewalk (item 9). In those cases you can be prosecuted for the injury even if the victim forgives you. Settlement still matters — it is weighed heavily in the outcome — but it no longer ends the case on its own.

Leaving the scene after injuring someone is a separate and much more serious matter. Stop, help, and give your details, even if the injury looks minor and even if you are worried about your visa.

→Your Visa

A fine notice for a helmet or a sidewalk is a small thing. A criminal case after an injury accident is not: a criminal fine or sentence can be weighed when you extend your stay, change status, or apply for permanent residence. If you have been in an accident on a scooter and the police have asked you to come in, get advice before the interview, not after.
