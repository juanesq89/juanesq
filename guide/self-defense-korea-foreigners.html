They Hit First: Self-Defense (정당방위) in Korea, and Why the Police Often Treat Both Sides as Suspects

By Juan, Attorney at Law 7 min read English · Español · Português

Someone shoves you outside a bar, you push back, and he falls. A man grabs your partner on the subway and you pull him off. A stranger swings at you and you swing back harder. In each case you will probably tell the police you were defending yourself. Whether the law agrees depends on one article of the Criminal Act and three words in it: present, unjust, and reasonable.

Many foreigners arrive with the idea that in Korea anyone who hits back is charged, whatever happened. That is not what the law says. Self-defense (정당방위, jeongdang bangwi) is a complete defence. But it has conditions, and they are applied to what can be proved afterwards: the CCTV, the injuries, and the order in which things happened. When two people end up exchanging blows, the police usually record both as suspects and let the evidence sort it out.

→What Article 21 Says

Article 21(1) of the Criminal Act: an act done to defend your own or another person's legal interests against a present, unjust infringement is not punished if there was reasonable cause for it (상당한 이유).

  • Present. The attack must be happening or about to happen. Hitting someone who has already stopped, or chasing someone who is walking away, is no longer defending against anything. It is a new act.
  • Unjust. You cannot claim self-defense against a lawful act, such as a police officer making a lawful arrest or a shop guard holding you for the police after a theft.
  • Reasonable cause. The response has to be a reasonable way of stopping the attack. Using a bottle against an open-handed push, or continuing after the other person is on the ground, is where most claims fail.
  • Defending someone else counts. The article covers another person's interests, so pulling an attacker off a friend or a stranger is judged the same way.

→When You Went Too Far: Articles 21(2) and 21(3)

If the defence exceeded what was reasonable, it is not a complete defence, but the court may reduce or remit the sentence depending on the circumstances (Art. 21(2)). And if the excess happened at night or in another unsettled situation, out of fear, shock, excitement or panic, the act is not punished (Art. 21(3)).

That last rule matters for the situations foreigners actually find themselves in: late at night, after drinking, outnumbered, in a place they do not know. It is not automatic. The circumstances have to be shown, which is why your first account to the police matters so much.

Two related rules exist. Necessity (긴급피난, Art. 22) covers acts to avoid a present danger. Self-help (자구행위, Art. 23) covers acts to secure a claim when the legal process could not do it in time. Both also require reasonable cause.

The question is never only who hit first. It is whether the attack was still happening, and whether your response was a reasonable way to stop it.

→The First 48 Hours

  1. Call 112 yourself. Being the person who called the police is part of your account.
  2. Ask for the CCTV to be kept. Bars, shops, buses and building management offices overwrite footage. Ask the police to secure it, and ask the business directly not to delete it.
  3. Photograph your own injuries and see a doctor, even if they seem minor. Injuries on your forearms and hands can show you were blocking.
  4. Get witnesses' names and numbers before they leave.
  5. Do not message the other person to argue about who started it. Anything you write can be used against you.

→At the Police Station

Since 2 October 2026 the prosecutor at the public prosecution office (공소청) decides whether to indict on the basis of the police file and does not question suspects, so the police interview is the statement that counts. Give the sequence clearly: what the other person did, what you did, and when it stopped. You have the right to an interpreter and to remain silent; see questioned by Korean police.

If the police conclude there is no crime, they send the file to the prosecutor with a written reason instead of referring the case for indictment (Criminal Procedure Act Art. 245-5(1)(ii)). The person who filed the complaint is notified within 7 days (Art. 245-6) and may object to the head of the police station within 3 months (Art. 245-7). A self-defense finding at the police stage is therefore not always the end.

The limitation period for prosecution is 5 years for simple assault and 7 years for injury (Criminal Procedure Act Art. 249(1)). While the case is open, a foreign suspect can face a departure ban (출국정지), and a conviction can affect visa renewals.
