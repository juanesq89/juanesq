Online Gambling (온라인 도박) Is Illegal in Korea — Even on Foreign Sites, Even If It's Legal Back Home

By Juan, Attorney at Law 6 min read English · Español · Português

You didn't leave Korea. You didn't touch a chip. You placed a bet on a sportsbook app licensed in another country, from your phone, in your apartment in Korea — and that alone can be a criminal offense here.

Foreigners in Korea routinely assume that if a gambling site is legally licensed somewhere else — Malta, Curaçao, the UK, their home country — using it from Korea can't be a Korean legal problem. That assumption is wrong, and it's one of the more common ways foreign residents end up facing a criminal investigation for something that felt, to them, like an ordinary weekend habit.

→Korea's Gambling Law Is Built Around Location, Not Licensing

Under Korea's Criminal Act (형법, hyeongbeop) and the National Sports Promotion Act (국민체육진흥법, gungmincheyukjinheungbeop), gambling (도박, dobak) is illegal in Korea unless it takes place through one of a short list of specifically authorized channels — the state sports-betting system (스포츠토토), horse and boat racing operated by public corporations, the lottery, and casinos licensed for foreigner-only or Gangwon Land use. Everything else is unauthorized, regardless of where the operator is based or licensed.

Korean prosecutors have consistently taken the position that the crime occurs where the bet is placed — meaning where you are physically located when you press "place bet" — not where the gambling company's servers or headquarters happen to sit. A site being 100% legal in the country that licensed it changes nothing about its legality for someone gambling from inside Korea.

→What Actually Gets Investigated

  • Offshore sports betting apps and sites — the single most common category, especially around major international sporting events
  • Overseas online casino platforms — slots, live-dealer tables, and card games accessed through a foreign-licensed site
  • Illegal domestic gambling sites disguised as "private" sports-toto or card-game platforms operating without authorization, often the largest cases by dollar volume
  • Crypto-based betting and casino platforms, which investigators treat the same as any other unauthorized gambling regardless of the payment method used

Investigations frequently start from the payment side, not the betting side — banks and card networks flag unusual transfer patterns to known gambling-adjacent payment processors, or a larger ring gets cracked and its full customer/user list becomes evidence against every individual bettor on it, foreign or Korean.

→Penalties — And Why "I Was Just a Player" Doesn't Fully Protect You

RoleTypical Exposure
Simple gambling (단순도박) — placing bets as a playerFine, generally up to around 10M KRW, though repeated or habitual gambling (상습도박) is treated far more seriously and can carry a prison sentence
Operating or facilitating a site (도박개장)Up to several years' imprisonment — far more severe, and can sweep in anyone who recruited other bettors or received a referral cut
Advertising an illegal gambling siteA separate criminal offense on its own

"Habitual" gambling (상습도박) is judged by pattern and frequency, not by how much money changed hands — a modest but regular betting habit can cross that line.

→The Foreign Factor: Visa Consequences on Top of the Criminal Case

  • A gambling conviction is a criminal record, which is disclosed and weighed in future visa renewals, F-5 permanent residency applications, and naturalization
  • Depending on visa type and the severity of the case, it can be a basis for visa cancellation or deportation
  • Even a case resolved with a fine and no prison time still shows up as a criminal disposition on background checks

→If You're Contacted by Police About Online Gambling

  1. Don't ignore a summons or notice. Failing to appear tends to make investigators — and later, a judge — view the case less favorably
  2. Don't make statements without preparing first. How much you bet, how often, and whether you referred others all affect whether this looks like simple gambling or something more serious
  3. Preserve your own records — transaction history, dates, and amounts — rather than deleting the app or account, which can look like evidence destruction
  4. Get a Korean attorney involved before the first formal interview, particularly if there's any chance the case could be characterized as habitual gambling or if you referred other users to the platform

The gap between "I bet on sports with an app I downloaded from an ad" and "criminal defendant in Korea" is smaller than most foreigners assume — because in Korean law, the location of the bettor, not the location or licensing of the platform, is what decides whether a crime occurred.
