Moving Out Before Your Lease Ends in Korea: When You Can Leave, and How the Deposit Comes Back

By Juan, Attorney at Law 7 min read English · Español · Português

A new job in another city, a transfer home, a relationship that ended. Plans change faster than two-year leases, and the question is always the same: can I just give notice and leave? In Korea the answer depends on which term of the lease you are in.

This guide covers leases of a home under the Housing Lease Protection Act (주택임대차보호법), whether you pay jeonse (전세) or monthly rent (월세). Shops and offices follow a different statute; see our commercial lease guide.

→In the First Term, There Is No Right to Leave Early

A lease for a fixed term binds both sides for that term. The Housing Lease Protection Act protects the tenant's right to stay; it does not give the tenant a general right to end the first term early because plans changed. If you move out halfway through a two-year lease without an agreement, the landlord can treat you as still bound by the lease, rent included, and will usually hold back the deposit while that is argued.

So in the first term there are three ways out:

  • A clause in the contract. Some leases include an early-termination clause (중도해지 특약), for example letting the tenant leave on a stated notice period. Read the special terms (특약사항) of your contract first.
  • The landlord's agreement. Most early exits happen this way: the landlord agrees to end the lease on a date, often once a new tenant has been found.
  • A serious breach by the landlord. If the landlord does not keep the home usable, you may have grounds to terminate. Whether a defect is serious enough depends on the facts, so get advice before relying on it. Leaks are covered in our water leak guide.

1A Lease Written for Less Than Two Years

A home lease with no fixed term, or a term shorter than two years, is treated as a two-year lease (Art. 4(1)). That rule exists to protect the tenant, so the same article lets the tenant choose to rely on the shorter term instead. If you signed a one-year lease, the landlord cannot make you stay a second year: you may leave when the year ends. The landlord, on the other hand, cannot use the one-year term to push you out; you may stay for two years if you wish.

This matters for students, language teachers and anyone on a one-year job contract. Tell the landlord in writing before the year ends that you are leaving on the agreed end date, so there is no argument later that the lease simply continued.

2After an Automatic Renewal: Notice, Then Three Months

If the landlord gives no notice of non-renewal or of changed terms between six months and two months before the lease ends, and the tenant gives no notice at least two months before the end, the lease renews automatically on the same terms (묵시적 갱신, Art. 6(1)). The renewed term is treated as two years (Art. 6(2)).

Once a lease has renewed automatically, the tenant can end it at any time. The termination takes effect three months after the landlord receives the notice.

That is Article 6-2. The three-month exit belongs to the tenant; the landlord does not get the same right during the renewed term. One exception: a tenant who has fallen two months' rent behind, or has otherwise seriously breached the lease, does not get the automatic renewal at all (Art. 6(3)).

Because the three months run from the day the landlord receives the notice, send it in a form that proves the date. A certified content letter (내용증명) is the safest; a text or KakaoTalk message the landlord answers is far better than a phone call.

3After Using Your Renewal Right: the Same Three Months

If you renewed by exercising your lease renewal right (계약갱신요구권, Art. 6-3), the renewed lease follows the same rule: Article 6-3(4) applies Article 6-2, so you can give notice at any time and the lease ends three months after the landlord receives it. Many tenants think they are locked into the second two years. They are not. How the renewal right itself works is in our lease renewal right guide.

4Negotiating an Early Exit in the First Term

With no clause and no renewal, leaving early is a negotiation. Landlords commonly ask the outgoing tenant to help find the next tenant and to cover the real estate agent's fee for the new lease. The law does not decide who pays that fee in an agreed early exit; it is whatever the two of you agree. How brokerage fees are calculated is in our brokerage fee guide.

Whatever you agree, put it in writing and have both sides sign it or confirm it by message:

  • The date the lease ends and the date you hand over the keys
  • The date and amount of the deposit return, and any deductions
  • Who pays the agent's fee for the next tenant, and how much
  • Rent and maintenance fees (관리비) up to the end date

"You can leave once we find someone," with no date attached, leaves you paying rent for an open-ended period. Ask for a fixed date.

5Getting the Deposit Back When You Leave

Even after the lease ends, the tenancy is treated as continuing until the deposit is returned (Art. 4(2)). The deposit and the handover of the home go together, so do not hand over the keys or move your address registration out before you are paid unless you have secured your position first. If the landlord will not pay on the end date, our deposit return guide explains the lease registration order (임차권등기명령) that lets you move out without losing priority.

If you are leaving Korea, arrange this before you fly. Someone in Korea holding your power of attorney can receive the deposit and handle the handover; chasing a deposit from abroad without one is much harder.

→What to Do Before You Give Notice

  1. Work out which term you are in: the first term, an automatic renewal, or a renewal under the renewal right. The contract date and any renewal messages tell you.
  2. Read the special terms (특약사항) for an early-termination clause.
  3. In a renewed term, send notice in a way that proves when the landlord received it, and count three months from that day.
  4. In the first term, agree an end date in writing before you sign for a new home.
  5. Do not hand over the keys or move your registration out before the deposit is paid or a lease registration order is in place.
  6. Leaving Korea? Sign a power of attorney before you go.
