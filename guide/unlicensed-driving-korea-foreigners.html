Your International Permit Ran Out Months Ago: Driving Without a License (무면허운전) in Korea

By Juan, Attorney at Law 7 min read English · Español · Português

You arrived on an international driving permit, a year went by, and you kept driving. Or you only ever had the license from home. Or your Korean license was suspended and you needed the car for one errand. At a routine checkpoint, all three end in the same charge: driving without a license (무면허운전, mumyeonheo unjeon).

Unlicensed driving is a crime in Korea, not a ticket. It is investigated by the police, it ends in a criminal record if you are fined, and it also stops you from getting a Korean license for a year afterwards. Most foreigners who are charged did not set out to drive illegally. They misread how long their permit lasted, or which documents count.

→What Counts as Driving Without a License

Article 43 of the Road Traffic Act forbids driving without a license issued in Korea or while that license is suspended. Article 152(1) punishes driving a car in that situation, and expressly includes driving on an international permit or recognised foreign license after it has expired or after you were banned from using it, with up to 1 year's imprisonment or a fine of up to 3 million won.

A license from your home country is not, on its own, a license to drive in Korea. What Article 96 recognises is an international driving permit issued under the 1949 Geneva or 1968 Vienna conventions or a bilateral agreement, or a foreign license that Korea recognises under a mutual recognition agreement. For electric kickboards and small scooters the details are in riding an electric scooter in Korea.

→The One-Year Clock on an International Permit

Article 96(1) lets you drive on an international permit or a recognised foreign license for one year from the date you entered Korea. The printed expiry date on the permit does not extend that. Two more limits are in the same article:

  • Only the vehicle categories printed on the permit. A permit marked for cars does not cover a motorbike.
  • No commercial passenger or freight vehicles (Art. 96(2)). Renting a car from a rental company is the exception the article allows.

If you are staying longer than a year, the safe path is to convert your foreign license before the year runs out. The process is in converting a foreign driver's license.

→If There Was an Accident

Normally, a driver who injures someone through careless driving is not prosecuted if the victim does not want punishment or if the car is covered by comprehensive insurance (Act on Special Cases concerning the Settlement of Traffic Accidents, Arts. 3(2) and 4(1)). Driving without a license is one of the listed exceptions (Art. 3(2), item 7). An injury caused by an unlicensed driver can be prosecuted even after a settlement and even with full insurance, under Article 3(1): up to 5 years' imprisonment without labour (금고) or a fine of up to 20 million won.

Do not leave the scene because you are worried about the license. Leaving turns a manageable case into a much heavier one, and it also lengthens the license bar to 5 years (Art. 82(2), item 1, proviso). See hit-and-run in Korea and the first 24 hours after a car accident.

The permit's printed expiry date is not your deadline. One year from the day you entered Korea is.

→The License Bar Afterwards

Apart from the criminal case, Article 82(2) bars you from obtaining a Korean license for a period counted from the day you drove:

  • 1 year after driving without a license (6 months if you are applying for a moped license) (item 1).
  • 2 years if you have driven without a license three or more times (item 2).
  • 5 years if you injured someone and left without helping or reporting (item 1, proviso).

During that period you also may not drive on an international permit (Art. 96(3)). There is one exception that matters: if the case ends in a suspended indictment (기소유예), a suspended sentence of judgment (선고유예) or a penalty lighter than a fine, you may obtain a license within the period (Art. 82(2), proviso). A fine, including one by summary order, does not qualify. What these outcomes mean is in three words that all translate as “suspended”.

→If You Are Stopped or Charged

Since 2 October 2026 the prosecutor at the public prosecution office (공소청) decides on the basis of the police file and does not question suspects, so the police interview is the statement that counts.

  1. Do not drive away from the checkpoint, and do not let a friend claim to have been driving. Both create new offences.
  2. Bring the paper trail: passport entry stamps or entry record, your permit and home license, and any conversion appointment you had booked. They show how the mistake happened.
  3. Check whether alcohol is also involved. Unlicensed driving and drink-driving are charged together. See DUI in Korea.
  4. Expect a fine by summary order in a simple first case, and know what it means before you pay: a summary order is a criminal conviction. Asking for a suspended indictment is worth it because of the license bar above.
  5. Think about your visa. A fine is recorded and is weighed in renewals and naturalisation. Deportation under Article 46(1)(xiii) of the Immigration Act applies to sentences of imprisonment (금고 or heavier), not to fines, but an accident case can reach that level. See deportation and entry bans.

The limitation period for prosecution under Article 152 is 5 years (Criminal Procedure Act Art. 249(1)(v)).
